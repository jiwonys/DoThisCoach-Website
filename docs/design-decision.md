# Design Decision: The Performance Weave

## Decision

DoThis will use **The Performance Weave** as the production experience.

The central visual is a responsive technical-fabric system. Training, sport, food, recovery, goals, and progress each apply tension to the same surface. DoThis does not erase those demands; it coordinates them into a clear seam named Today.

## Why this direction won

The Performance Weave was the only prototype that simultaneously achieved the three non-negotiable outcomes:

1. **Original identity:** it does not depend on a glowing orb, generic particles, a sci-fi dashboard, floating devices, or a decorative 3D model. The material system could belong only to an athlete-performance product built around connected context.
2. **Product comprehension:** the visitor sees fragmented inputs pull on one shared surface, then sees the system reorganize after “I have volleyball tonight at 7 PM.” The website demonstrates the product promise without pretending to perform a live medical or training decision.
3. **Conversion clarity:** semantic copy and the App Store CTA remain above the fold while the scene adds meaning behind them. The real Today and Workout screens appear legibly within the material world and again in the proof chapter.

Concept B was the clearest literal explanation of schedule reorganization and will inform the physical motion of the Adaptive Day chapter. It did not win because its colored pieces felt closer to planning software than a distinctive DoThis brand system.

Concept C created the most cinematic progress metaphor. It did not win because procedural terrain and route markers were less specific to DoThis, implied an outdoor/running bias, and provided less product clarity for comparable GPU cost.

## Production translation

The selected production system will retain:

- one persistent WebGL Canvas on the marketing homepage;
- a custom tension-and-reveal shader grounded in technical athletic fabric;
- a central Today seam that reorganizes when contextual demands change;
- real DoThis screenshots on readable spatial planes;
- GSAP-owned chapter timelines and Lenis-owned scroll behavior;
- semantic HTML for all essential copy, navigation, CTAs, pricing, and legal facts;
- device-specific camera and geometry tiers rather than scaled desktop coordinates;
- a static DOM/CSS fallback and a complete reduced-motion presentation;
- a visually calmer Premium Preview and conversion chapter.

The production build will not retain the three prototype routes or prototype-only tuning interface. Experimental source will be removed after the selected architecture is migrated.

## Motion language

The production motion language is:

- **Controlled:** transitions decelerate with weight and stop deliberately.
- **Athletic:** the material loads, releases, and redirects rather than floating.
- **Responsive:** pointer and touch create bounded local pressure.
- **Precise:** product reveals and typography masks resolve crisply.
- **Elastic:** context changes redistribute tension without cartoon bounce.

## Success criteria

The direction remains successful only if production QA confirms that:

- the first viewport explains what DoThis does;
- the adaptive-day interaction remains understandable without WebGL;
- screenshots remain readable;
- the App Store path remains obvious;
- mobile stays intentionally composed;
- the defining surface meets the documented frame and asset budgets;
- article, support, privacy, and terms experiences remain lightweight and stable.
