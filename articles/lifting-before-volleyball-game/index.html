Sport-day training

Lifting Before a Volleyball Game: What to Cut and What to Keep

Match day does not have to be a rest day. The question is not whether to lift, but which qualities to train and how much fatigue you are willing to carry onto the court.

Published August 19, 2026Updated August 19, 202611 min read
The short answer

You can lift on match day if the session is short, sits at least six hours before first serve, and avoids heavy lower-body work taken close to failure. Keep upper-body and trunk work, keep loads well short of a limit, and move the heavy squats and hinges to the day after the match instead.

Who this plan is for

This is for recreational and league volleyball players who lift and do not want to choose between the two. It assumes you are healthy and already training.

  • League players with a fixed weekly match night.
  • Lifters who do not want a training block interrupted by every game.
  • Athletes who feel flat on court and suspect their lifting is the cause.
  • Anyone whose plan currently schedules leg day and match day together by accident.

Weekly schedule

The useful unit is the week, not the day. Place the heaviest lower-body session as far from the match as the week allows, and treat the match itself as real training load rather than a gap in the plan.

Warm-up

On match day the warmup is the session's main safety feature. Raise temperature, move through range, then rehearse the movements the match will demand.

Progression rules

In-season progression is slower on purpose. The match is the performance; the lifting exists to support it.

  1. Progress the day-after session normally: add reps inside the range, then add the smallest available load.
  2. Do not progress the match-day session at all. It stays easy by design.
  3. If your first two matches of a block feel flat, remove the match-day session before you remove the heavy day.
  4. Deload the lower-body day in any week with two matches.

Weekly placement and recovery

  • Treat the match as a hard training session in your weekly total, not as a rest day.
  • Expect calves and adductors to be the first areas to complain during a jumping block.
  • Sleep and food do more for match-day readiness than any pre-match routine.
  • Persistent jump pain in the front of the knee is a reason to reduce jumping volume and seek assessment, not to stretch harder.

Common programming questions

How many hours before a match can I lift?

Six or more is a reasonable buffer for an easy upper-body session. Heavy lower-body work needs far longer, which is why it belongs on a different day.

Will skipping leg day before matches cost me strength?

No. Strength is built across weeks. Moving one session by a day changes almost nothing about a training block.

What if my only free gym time is match day?

Then lift on match day and keep it easy and upper-body focused. Consistency at a lower dose beats an ideal plan you cannot attend.

Safety and limitations

Stop if an exercise produces sharp pain, dizziness, chest pain, or symptoms that feel unsafe. Persistent joint pain, swelling, or instability deserves assessment by a qualified clinician rather than a heavier warmup.
