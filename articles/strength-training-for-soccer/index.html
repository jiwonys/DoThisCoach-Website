Strength Training for Soccer: Hamstrings, Groin, and Repeat Sprints

Hamstring and groin strains account for a large share of missed soccer time. A plan that ignores eccentric hamstring and adductor work is missing the point.

Published August 6, 2026Updated August 6, 202611 min read
The short answer

Lift twice a week around training and matches. Include eccentric hamstring work and adductor loading every week without exception, and place heavy legs away from match day.

Who this plan is for

This is for outfield players at any competitive level who train or play several times a week. Goalkeepers should keep the same lower body and add more overhead work.

  • Players with a history of hamstring or groin strains.
  • Anyone whose sprint speed fades in the last twenty minutes.
  • Players returning from a layoff who need a re-entry structure.
  • Adults playing recreationally who train less than they play.

Weekly schedule

Lift on the day after a match or on a training day, never the day before a match. Two sessions is the realistic ceiling once matches and team training are on the calendar.

The soccer strength plan

The hamstring and adductor work is the non-negotiable part. If time runs out, cut something else.

Session A - Heavy and eccentric

The main session. Hamstring work goes early while you are fresh enough to control it.

ExerciseSetsTargetRestCoaching note
Nordic Hamstring Curl35 reps2 minLower as slowly as you can control; catch with the hands. Soreness is expected in the first weeks.
Barbell Back Squat45 reps3 minControl the descent and drive out without the hips shooting up first.
Copenhagen Plank320 sec each side90 secBuild the adductor before it gets loaded in a tackle.
Romanian Deadlift38 reps2 minHips back, hamstrings loaded, back steady.
Side Plank230 sec each side60 secHold the position rather than chasing the clock.

Session B - Single-leg and trunk

Shorter and lighter, fitted after team training.

Hip Adduction310-12 reps90 secSecond adductor exposure of the week.

Progression rules

Nordic curls progress by range and control, not by load. Everything else uses the usual small increases.

  1. Start Nordics at whatever range you can genuinely control and extend the range over weeks.
  2. Add small loads on squats and Romanian deadlifts when all sets are clean at the top of the range.
  3. In a congested fixture run, keep the hamstring and adductor work and drop the rest.
  4. Never introduce Nordics for the first time in the same week as a match. Start them in a lighter week.

Weekly placement and recovery

  • Nordic soreness in the first two weeks is normal and settles quickly; do not stop the exercise because of it.
  • A hamstring that feels tight before a match is a warning, not something to stretch through.
  • Two lifting sessions plus team training plus a match is a full week. Adding a third session is usually where players break down.

Common programming questions

Do Nordics really matter?

Eccentric hamstring work is one of the few strength interventions with a consistent record against hamstring strains. It is the exercise least worth skipping.

Can I lift the day before a match?

Not heavy. If it is the only slot you have, halve the volume and remove the eccentric work.

What if I already strained a hamstring?

That needs a clinician and a graded return, not this template. Come back to the plan once you are cleared to sprint.

Safety and limitations

Stop if an exercise produces sharp pain, dizziness, or chest pain. An acute hamstring or groin strain requires assessment and a structured return to sprinting rather than working around it.
