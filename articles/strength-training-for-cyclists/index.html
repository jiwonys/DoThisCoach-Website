Strength Training for Cyclists: Two Sessions That Do Not Cost You Watts

Cycling builds enormous aerobic capacity and almost no bone or upper-body load. Lifting fills the gap without adding meaningful weight at two sessions a week.

Published August 9, 2026Updated August 9, 202611 min read
The short answer

Lift twice a week in the base period and once in race season. Go heavy with low reps, keep total volume small, and never put heavy legs the day before a quality ride.

Who this plan is for

This suits road, gravel, and mountain cyclists who ride several times a week. It assumes the bike is the priority.

  • Cyclists worried about bone density from years of non-impact training.
  • Riders who fade on repeated climbs rather than on steady efforts.
  • Anyone whose back or neck hurts on long rides.
  • Masters riders trying to hold power as they age.

Weekly schedule

The cyclist strength plan

Low reps and heavy loads build force with minimal soreness. High-rep leg work produces the fatigue riders complain about and adds little the bike is not already doing.

Progression rules

Add load, not reps. The whole point of low reps here is to build force while leaving the legs available for riding.

  1. Add a small load when all sets hit the top of the range with identical technique.
  2. Keep sets to four or fewer per exercise; more volume mostly buys soreness.
  3. As race season starts, hold the loads and drop to one session a week.
  4. If ride quality drops for two sessions in a row, the lifting is misplaced in the week rather than too heavy.

Weekly placement and recovery

  • Expect two to three weeks of leg soreness when starting, then very little. Begin a lifting block in an easier riding week.
  • Do not start lifting in the four weeks before a goal event.
  • Upper-body work costs almost nothing in ride quality and can stay in year round.

Common programming questions

Will lifting make me heavier?

Two low-volume sessions a week build force well before size. Most cyclists gain little or no weight and hold power better late in efforts.

Should I lift during race season?

One session a week at maintained loads keeps nearly everything and costs almost nothing.

Why low reps?

Heavy low-rep work builds force with far less muscle damage than high-rep leg work, which means the next ride is unaffected.

Safety and limitations

Stop if an exercise produces sharp pain, dizziness, or chest pain. Knee pain that appears on the bike after starting lifting usually means loads rose too quickly.
