Strength Training for Swimmers: Shoulders That Last a Season

Swimmers already do enormous pulling volume in the water. Useful dryland adds what the pool cannot: external rotation strength, vertical pulling, and lower-body force.

Published August 7, 2026Updated August 7, 202611 min read
The short answer

Lift twice a week away from hard pool sessions. Emphasise upper back, external rotation, and legs, and resist the urge to add more internal rotation volume to a shoulder that already gets plenty.

Who this plan is for

This is for competitive and masters swimmers with a regular pool schedule. It assumes the water is the priority and dryland is support.

  • Swimmers with recurring shoulder soreness late in a training block.
  • Athletes whose starts and turns are weaker than their swimming.
  • Masters swimmers who do nothing outside the pool.
  • Anyone whose dryland has been push-ups and nothing else.

Warm-up

Shoulders first. A few minutes of external rotation and scapular work before loading is the difference between a durable season and a sore one.

The swimmer dryland plan

Pulling volume in the gym should never exceed what the shoulder can recover from alongside the pool. When in doubt, cut the pulling and keep the rotator work.

Session A - Legs and posterior shoulder

The heavier day. Legs get trained here because starts and turns are pure lower body.

Progression rules

Progress legs freely. Progress upper body cautiously, because the pool is already supplying a large upper-body load.

  1. Add load on squats and Romanian deadlifts when every set is clean at the top of the range.
  2. Keep external rotation work light and strict; heavier is not better here.
  3. During a heavy pool block, hold dryland loads and cut a set.
  4. In taper week, keep one short session at half volume so the patterns stay familiar.

Weekly placement and recovery

  • Shoulder soreness that lasts more than two days means the gym pulling volume is competing with the pool, not complementing it.
  • Legs will be sore for the first two weeks and that will affect push-offs; start a block in a lighter pool week.
  • Do not add dryland volume in the same week the pool volume increases.

Common programming questions

Should swimmers do more pull-ups?

Some vertical pulling helps, but adding large volumes on top of pool pulling is the fastest route to a sore shoulder. Balance it with external rotation and upper back work.

Do swimmers need to squat?

Yes. Starts and turns are decided by leg force, and they are a meaningful share of a race.

How close to a meet can I lift?

Keep a short, light session early in meet week and drop heavy legs entirely in the final days.

Safety and limitations

Stop if an exercise produces sharp pain, dizziness, or chest pain. Shoulder pain that persists past a week, or that changes your stroke, needs a clinician rather than more rotator cuff work.
