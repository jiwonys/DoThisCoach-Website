How to Choose Your Starting Weight on Any Lift

Guessing your starting weight badly costs a week either way. A short ramp of practice sets tells you the answer in about five minutes.

Published August 16, 2026Updated August 16, 20268 min read
The short answer

Ramp up in two or three progressively heavier practice sets and pick the load where you could complete the target reps with two or three good reps still in reserve. When in doubt, go lighter; you will find the right weight in two sessions either way.

Who this plan is for

This is for anyone starting a new exercise, returning after time off, or beginning a program that does not tell them what to load.

  • Beginners facing an empty bar with no idea what to add.
  • Lifters starting an exercise they have never done.
  • People returning after months away.
  • Anyone whose program says 'work up to a hard set of eight' without explaining how.

The ramp method

Move up in decreasing jumps as you approach a hard set. The last practice set should feel clearly easier than the working set will.

Progression rules

Once the starting weight is set, ordinary progression rules apply and the initial guess stops mattering entirely.

  1. If you completed the target reps with four or more left in reserve, add roughly ten percent next session.
  2. If you fell short of the target reps, reduce by roughly ten percent next session.
  3. If you hit the target with two or three left in reserve, the weight is correct. Stay and progress from there.
  4. For upper body, the smallest useful jump is often 2.5lb per side. Fractional plates are worth owning.
  5. Never fix a bad guess mid-set by dropping the reps and grinding. Finish, adjust next time.

Weekly placement and recovery

  • Starting too light costs one session. Starting far too heavy can cost a week and a strained something.
  • New exercises feel harder than they are for the first two sessions because the movement is unfamiliar, not because you are weak at it.
  • After a layoff, start at roughly seventy percent of your previous weights regardless of how you feel on day one.

Common programming questions

Should I test a one-rep max to set my weights?

Not as a beginner. A maximal attempt on an unfamiliar lift carries real risk and tells you less than three sessions of ordinary training.

What does 'reps in reserve' mean?

How many additional good reps you could have completed. Two or three in reserve is the usual target for productive training.

Is starting light a waste of time?

No. Two easy sessions buy you months of uninterrupted progression, and the load catches up within a fortnight.

Safety and limitations

Stop if an exercise produces sharp pain, dizziness, or chest pain. Use a rack with safety pins or a spotter whenever failing a rep would trap you under the bar.
