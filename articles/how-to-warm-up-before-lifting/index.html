How to Warm Up Before Lifting: Five Minutes That Change the Session

A good warm-up is short, specific, and mostly consists of lighter versions of the exercise you are about to do.

Published August 1, 2026Updated August 1, 20268 min read
The short answer

Spend about five minutes raising temperature and moving the joints you are about to load, then ramp to your working weight in two or three progressively heavier practice sets. Skip long static stretching before heavy lifting.

Who this plan is for

This is for anyone who either skips warming up entirely or spends twenty minutes on it without knowing why.

  • Lifters whose first working set always feels worst.
  • People who have been told to stretch before lifting and are unsure.
  • Anyone short on time looking for what to cut.
  • Trainees with a joint that needs longer to feel ready.

The same structure every session: general, then specific. The specific part is the one that matters most and the one most often skipped.

The specific warm-up

Ramp sets are practice, not training. They should never be logged as working sets and should never accumulate fatigue.

The pattern for any main lift. Jumps get smaller as you approach the working weight.

Progression rules

The warm-up does not progress. It scales with the working weight and with how you feel that day.

  1. Heavier working weights need more ramp sets, not longer general warm-ups.
  2. On a day when something feels off, add a ramp set rather than adding stretching.
  3. Later exercises in a session need one light set at most; you are already warm.
  4. If the warm-up leaves you tired, it has become a workout and needs cutting.

Weekly placement and recovery

  • Static stretching before heavy lifting can temporarily reduce force production and does not reduce injury risk. Save it for after, or for its own session.
  • A cold session in a cold room needs longer; adjust rather than pushing through.
  • The first working set feeling bad and the second feeling fine is a warm-up problem, not a strength problem.

Common programming questions

Should I stretch before lifting?

Not statically. Move through ranges instead. Long held stretches before heavy work can temporarily reduce force and do not prevent injury.

How long should a warm-up take?

About five minutes of general work plus ramp sets on the first lift. Longer is usually wasted, shorter usually shows up in the first working set.

Do I need to warm up for every exercise?

One light set for later exercises is plenty. The full ramp is only needed for the first heavy lift of the session.

Safety and limitations

Stop if an exercise produces sharp pain, dizziness, or chest pain. Pain that a warm-up does not resolve is information worth acting on rather than warming up harder.
