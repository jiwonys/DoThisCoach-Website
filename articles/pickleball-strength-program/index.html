Sport-specific strength

Pickleball Strength Program: A 3-Day Plan for Court Durability

Pickleball is played in short lateral bursts with repeated deceleration. A useful strength program trains the tissues that absorb those stops, not just the ones that produce force forward.

Published August 19, 2026Updated August 19, 202613 min read
The short answer

Train three days per week: one lower-body strength day, one upper-body and trunk day, and one lateral power and durability day. Prioritize single-leg strength, calf and Achilles capacity, and shoulder external rotation, because those are the structures that fail under repeated stop-and-change demand.

Who this plan is for

This is written for recreational pickleball players, including those returning to sport after years away from structured training. It is a general plan, not treatment for an existing injury.

  • Players adding a third or fourth session per week and feeling it in the calves.
  • Adults returning to competitive sport after a long break from lifting.
  • Players who want to keep playing without a recurring Achilles or shoulder complaint.
  • Anyone whose current gym plan ignores lateral movement entirely.

The three-day pickleball program

Loads should allow two or three clean repetitions in reserve. Court sport already supplies plenty of high-intensity exposure.

Day 1 - Lower-body strength

Single-leg work dominates because the sport is played on one leg far more often than two.

Bulgarian Split Squat38 each side2 minKeep the torso quiet and drive through the front heel.
Trap Bar Deadlift36 reps2 minStop the set when back position starts to change.
Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift38 each side90 secMove slowly; balance is part of the training effect.
Copenhagen Plank215-20 sec each side60 secBegin from the knee version and progress slowly.
Standing Calf Raise312 reps90 secPause at the top and lower under control.

Day 2 - Upper body and trunk

Overhead serves and volleys make shoulder capacity a durability issue, not a vanity one.

Day 3 - Lateral power and calves

The session that most directly matches the sport. Keep total contacts low and quality high.

Progression rules

Strength days progress with load. Power days progress with quality and only then with contacts.

  1. Add reps inside the range first, then the smallest available load increase.
  2. On the power day, add contacts only when every landing is quiet and controlled.
  3. Cap lateral bounds at roughly 40 total contacts per session in the first month.
  4. Reduce the power day in any week with a tournament or more than three play sessions.

Weekly placement and recovery

  • Calves and Achilles take the most new load when play frequency rises. Increase play volume gradually.
  • One full rest day per week is reasonable, especially for players over 40 returning to sport.
  • Shoulder soreness after a serving-heavy day is common; sharp pain during the serve is not.
  • If ankle or Achilles pain persists beyond a few days, reduce jumping and lateral volume and seek assessment.

Common programming questions

Is pickleball enough exercise on its own?

It is genuine activity, but it does not build strength in the tissues that absorb deceleration. Strength training is what raises tolerance for the sport.

Why so much calf work?

Repeated split steps and lateral pushes load the calf and Achilles heavily. Building capacity there is one of the more useful things a court athlete can do.

I am over 50. Is this still appropriate?

The structure holds. Start at the lower end of every range, progress more slowly, and give yourself more recovery between hard days.

Safety and limitations

Stop if an exercise produces sharp pain, dizziness, chest pain, or symptoms that feel unsafe. Sudden calf or Achilles pain during play warrants prompt medical assessment rather than continued play.
