Strength Training for Basketball: Off-Season and In-Season Plans

Basketball punishes ankles, knees, and the ability to decelerate. The lifting that helps most is heavy hips, hard landings, and enough calf work to survive the volume.

Published August 5, 2026Updated August 5, 202612 min read
The short answer

Lift three times a week in the off-season and twice in-season. Prioritise heavy hinge and squat patterns, single-leg work, and landing mechanics over machine isolation.

Who this plan is for

This suits players from high school through recreational adult leagues who have access to a normal gym. It assumes a season with two or more games a week.

  • Players who jump a lot and land badly.
  • Anyone with recurring ankle sprains or patellar tendon soreness.
  • Guards who need to change direction without losing the hip.
  • Players who lose their legs by the fourth quarter.

The basketball strength plan

Landing quality ends a set before fatigue does. If the knees start collapsing inward on contact, that set is over.

Session A - Heavy lower and landing

The most important session of the week. Do not skip it when the schedule gets tight.

ExerciseSetsTargetRestCoaching note
Barbell Back Squat45 reps3 minFull control on the way down; this is not a bounce.
Depth Drop to Stick35 reps2 minLand quiet, absorb through the hips, hold the position for a full second.
Romanian Deadlift38 reps2 minHamstring capacity is what protects the knee on deceleration.

Progression rules

Add load in the off-season and hold it in-season. Trying to set records in February is how players end the year hurt.

  1. Off-season: add a small load whenever every set hits the top of the range cleanly.
  2. In-season: keep the same loads and cut sets. Maintenance is the goal, not improvement.
  3. Drop the jumping first when the game load rises. Keep the heavy hinge.
  4. If vertical drops across two weeks, the problem is total load, not insufficient training.

Weekly placement and recovery

  • Two hard lower-body days plus games is already a lot; the third session is the one to cut.
  • Ankle soreness that persists past 48 hours means the calf volume has outrun the tissue.
  • Sleep does more for jumping than any accessory exercise in this plan.

Common programming questions

Will lifting hurt my shot?

Not at these volumes. Shooting suffers when players are exhausted, which is a scheduling problem rather than a lifting one.

How do I lift during a heavy game week?

One session, on a game day after the game, at the same loads with half the sets.

Should I train jumping every session?

No. Jumping is a quality, not a conditioning tool. Three to four hard efforts is plenty.

Safety and limitations

Stop if an exercise produces sharp pain, dizziness, or chest pain. Persistent knee or ankle pain, and any ankle sprain that keeps recurring, needs a clinician rather than more volume.
