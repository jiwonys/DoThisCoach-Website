Strength Training for Golf: Rotation, Hips, and Clubhead Speed

Clubhead speed comes from the ground up. The gym work that moves it is heavy lower body plus the ability to resist rotation, not endless twisting drills.

Published August 8, 2026Updated August 8, 202610 min read
The short answer

Lift twice a week. Build lower-body force, train the trunk to resist rotation rather than only produce it, and keep hips and thoracic spine moving well.

Who this plan is for

This is for golfers of any handicap who want more speed and fewer back complaints. It assumes access to a normal gym.

  • Golfers whose low back aches after eighteen holes.
  • Players who have plateaued on distance.
  • Anyone who has been told they lack hip or thoracic rotation.
  • Golfers over forty who have never lifted seriously.

Two sessions a week fits around a normal playing schedule. Avoid heavy lower body the day before a round that matters.

The two-day golf plan

Speed work is quality work. When the medicine ball throws slow down, that part of the session is finished regardless of the set count.

Session A - Lower body force

The ground is where the swing starts. This is the session that changes distance.

Progression rules

Strength lifts progress by load. Throws progress by intent, never by adding more reps.

  1. Add small loads on the trap bar and hip thrust when all sets are clean.
  2. Keep medicine ball throws at four hard reps per side; more reps make them slower, which defeats the purpose.
  3. In peak playing season, drop to one session a week at the same loads rather than stopping entirely.
  4. If the low back is sore after lifting, reduce the rotation volume and add anti-rotation work instead.

Weekly placement and recovery

  • Walking eighteen holes is real training load; count it when planning the week.
  • Do not lift heavy legs the day before a competitive round.
  • Low back stiffness that only appears after golf usually reflects hip mobility and trunk endurance rather than a back problem.

Common programming questions

Will lifting make me tight and cost me distance?

Training through a full range does not reduce mobility. Golfers usually lose range from inactivity rather than from lifting.

How fast will clubhead speed change?

Most golfers see meaningful change over a few months, not weeks, and the lower-body strength work drives most of it.

Should I lift in season?

Yes, at reduced volume. One session a week keeps almost everything you built.

Safety and limitations

Stop if an exercise produces sharp pain, dizziness, or chest pain. Low back pain that radiates into the leg needs a clinician rather than more core work.
