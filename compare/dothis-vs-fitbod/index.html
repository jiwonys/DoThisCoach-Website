What Fitbod does better

Fitbod publishes a wider platform list than DoThis, including iPad, Apple Watch, and Android, and names integrations with Apple Health, Strava, and Fitbit. If you track across several services or want the workout on your wrist, that is a real advantage and DoThis does not currently match it.

What DoThis does differently

DoThis is built around a competition schedule. You tell it when you play, and it places lifting so a heavy leg session does not land the day before a match, cuts volume when fixtures pile up, and keeps durability work in the plan when the week gets tight.

Which one fits you

Choose Fitbod if

you train in a gym without a fixed competition calendar, want Apple Watch or Android, or already live inside Strava and Fitbit.

Choose DoThis if

you play a sport on a schedule and want lifting placed around matches, and you want to import a plan a coach already gave you.

Questions

Do both apps adjust to my equipment?

Yes. Both publish equipment customisation. Fitbod describes settings for bodyweight, bands, and limited gear; DoThis rebuilds the session around what you have that day.

Which is cheaper?

On the figures listed above, DoThis Premium Monthly and Fitbod's lower monthly tier are both $12.99. Fitbod's listed annual tiers are lower than DoThis at $79.99 and $95.99 against $119.99. Check current prices before deciding.

Can I move my history over?

We cannot verify an export path from Fitbod, so assume you would start fresh. DoThis can import a workout you paste in, which is not the same as importing a training history.
